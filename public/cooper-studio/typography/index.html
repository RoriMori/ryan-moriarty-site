DM Sans — Primary Recommendation

Why this font earns its place on an architect's portfolio.

Recommended typeface
DM Sans
A geometric sans-serif with measured proportions and quiet authority. Designed for digital interfaces, naturally at home in precise, considered environments.
Aesthetic fit
DM Sans carries the same design values Cam described: minimal, neutral, structured. Its geometry feels architectural — grids, proportions, restraint. It doesn't perform. It holds space.
Web performance
Designed specifically for screen rendering. Excellent legibility at small sizes. Hosted on Google Fonts — no self-hosting required, fast CDN delivery, zero additional cost.
Weight range
300 (Light) through 700 (Bold) with optical sizing support. The 300–500 range is the sweet spot for this site: thin enough to be refined, readable enough for body copy at any size.

Instrument Sans — Alternate 1

A warmer, more humanist alternative with strong editorial character.

Alternate option
Instrument Sans
Developed by Rodrigo Fuenzalida and used extensively by Figma, Instrument Sans sits between geometric and humanist — slightly warmer than DM Sans, with subtle optical corrections that give it more personality at display sizes. Strong editorial feel.
vs. DM Sans
Warmer — slightly more expressive
More optical — less purely geometric
Bolder presence at large sizes
Same Google Fonts delivery
Strengths
+More distinctive at headline sizes — gives the site a stronger visual signature
+Humanist details make long-form text (process, about) feel more considered
+Pairs beautifully with International Orange — the warmth complements
+Used on high-end creative and design sites — positioned well for the audience
Considerations
−Slightly more opinionated — may compete with the work if not set carefully
−Weight 300 is less readable at small sizes than DM Sans Light
−Newer font — less tested across older browsers and OS rendering

Geist — Alternate 2

Vercel's type system: technical precision with quiet confidence.

Alternate option
Geist
Designed by Vercel for developer and technical interfaces, Geist is unusually clean for a sans-serif — near-zero ink traps, extremely consistent stroke width, excellent at both display and caption sizes. The technical precision makes it an interesting choice for an architect who values exactness.
vs. DM Sans
More technical — sharper, colder
More consistent stroke width
Less warm — needs careful pairing
Same Google Fonts delivery
Strengths
+Extremely precise — mirrors the rigor of technical architectural drawing
+Exceptional at small sizes — captions and metadata read with unusual clarity
+Strong differentiation — not commonly used on architecture sites
+International Orange accent pops harder against its cooler neutrality
Considerations
−Can read as "software product" rather than "design practice" if not handled carefully
−Less warmth — the "quiet" Cam asked for could tip toward cold
−Newer font, less established rendering history across all devices

Outfit — Alternate 3

The most approachable option: geometric warmth with broad appeal.

Alternate option
Outfit
A geometric sans-serif with rounder terminals and friendlier proportions than DM Sans. More accessible in tone, less austere. A good choice if Cam wants the site to feel welcoming to residential clients who may not have a design background — the warmth lowers the barrier without sacrificing clarity.
vs. DM Sans
Warmer — rounder, more approachable
Less austere — friendlier proportions
More accessible for non-design audience
Same Google Fonts delivery
Strengths
+Most accessible option — residential clients will find it familiar and inviting
+Geometric but warm — complements International Orange well
+Excellent at body copy weights — long project descriptions read naturally
+Variable font — single file, great performance
Considerations
−Least distinctive of the four — trades precision for warmth
−Can feel more "startup" than "studio" at larger sizes if not set tightly
−May not signal the level of design rigor Cam wants to convey to collaborators and firms
